# hourly-unit-scale

Status: Draft · Pass 0 · Updated 2026-11-23 · Linear: OPS-630

> What this is: the journal DB's hourly functions stop selecting readings
> by a unit literal and read each channel's scale from a table generated
> from the sema snapshot, so a channel recorded in one unit enum and the
> same channel recorded in another, by era or by derivation, read as the
> same quantity.

## The fault

Status: Accepted · Pass 1 · Updated 2026-11-16

`gridworks.calc_energy_1hr` and `gridworks.calc_hourly_data` (source:
gridworks-data `src/gw_data/db/scripts/misc/create_udf_*.sql`) pick
readings with `channel_name = '...' AND unit = '...'`, one literal per
channel. The journal records a DataChannel's unit in
`spaceheat.telemetry.name` (`GpmTimes100`) and a DerivedChannel's in
`gw1.unit` (`GpmX100`). Maple's `primary-flow` is the fleet's one derived
flow channel, so its hourly `hp_kwh_th` came out empty (OPS-608). Oak's
`sieg-send-flow` was in the same enum until 2026-10-23. Any derived
channel that stands in for a measured one meets the same literal.

A measured channel whose unit changed between layout eras meets it too,
and the journal shows two such boundaries at every house. Before
2026-01-09 the tank and buffer depths are `WaterTempCTimes1000`; the
function wants `FahrenheitX100`, so the hourly tank columns are empty
before that date. From the roll to the new main (2026-10-23 to 10-29,
by house) `hp-lwt`, `hp-ewt`, `dist-swt`, `dist-rwt` and
`store-cold-pipe` are `CelsiusTimes100`; the function wants
`WaterTempCTimes1000`, so hp, dist and store temperatures and energies
are empty after it. The fix is the same join.

The one-second reader under `calc_energy_1hr`
(`create_udf_retrieve_readings_1s.sql`) gap-fills each `(channel, unit)`
series on its own and holds a series' last value to the end of the
window. With one literal per channel this meant that after a house's
roll the old spelling's last reading stood in for the live one, so the
post-roll `hp_kwh_th` and `dist_kwh` on prod are stale values, not
empty cells. With both spellings read, the stale series would be
averaged with the live one instead. The fix is a reader that treats a
channel's rows as one lineage: one row per channel per second, the
latest reading with that reading's unit.

On 2026-10-26 the production `calc_energy_1hr` was edited in place to
`unit IN ('GpmTimes100', 'GpmX100')` for primary-flow. The repo file has
the single literal; nothing else differs. The journal-DB rule is one SQL
script in gridworks-data, tried on `tsdb_tap`, then run on prod, so step
1 below is the repo catching up with what is deployed.

## The table

Status: Accepted · Pass 1 · Updated 2026-11-16

`gridworks.unit_scale`, one row per `(unit_type, unit)` of the two unit
enums the journal records (`gw1.unit` for derived channels,
`spaceheat.telemetry.name` for measured ones): the `quantity`, a
`gw1.quantity` value (Temperature, FlowRate, Power, Energy, WindSpeed,
Irradiance, Currency, …) given by the two published projection words,
`gw1.unit.quantity.projection` 001 and
`spaceheat.telemetry.quantity.projection` 001, through their generated
`project()` in the vendored snapshot; the `base_unit` the stored
integer is a multiple of (C, F, gpm, W, Wh, kWh, mph, W/m², $/MWh); and
the `scale`, so `value / scale` is in `base_unit`. Both are needed
because one quantity arrives in several bases: temperature as
`WaterTempCTimes1000` and `CelsiusTimes100` (°C) and as `FahrenheitX100`
and `FahrenheitX10` (°F), energy as `WattHours` and `KilowattHoursX1000`.
A state enum's name also sits in `unit_type` (`relay.closed.or.open`,
`gw1.main.auto.state`); those channels have no row and the functions
read them as they do today.

The table is generated by a script in gridworks-data from the vendored
sema snapshot and is the script's alone to write. The snapshot grows by
the two projection words and `gw1.quantity` (the seed request names
only `g.node.gt` and two enums today), so the quantity column is the
vocabulary's, never a string the generator invents. The functions
select by the `gw1.quantity` value name. `scale` and
`base_unit` come from the enum value's name by the convention the
`gw1.unit` enum states ("a scaled unit carries one decimal digit more
precision than the accuracy of the best source"): `(X|Times)(\d+)` is
the scale, 1 where absent, and a short map in the generator gives the
base for the names that do not spell it (`PowerW` → W, `WaterTempC…` →
C, `AirTempF…` → F). The map is hand-kept with a note naming the missing
word: nothing in sema states a unit's scale or base unit, only its
quantity. A test asserts that every `(unit_type, unit)` in
`reading_channels` whose quantity a hourly column reads resolves to a
row.

The hourly functions join on `(unit_type, unit)` and select by
`channel_name` and quantity, converting from `base_unit` to the
column's unit: `primary_flow_gpm` is the average of `value / scale`
where the channel is `primary-flow` and the quantity is FlowRate;
`hp_avg_lwt` is the average in °C, converted to °F by the existing
`C2F` where `base_unit` is C. Every unit literal in both functions goes.
`readings_1hr` and `retrieve_readings_1s` already expose `unit_type`
beside `unit`, so no view changes.

## Hourly channels

The hourly columns are channels. `cached_hourly_data` is a 39-column
pivot whose units live in column names (`oat_f`, `ws_mph`) and whose
schema has to match a function's return table by hand; every column is
a quantity computed from named input channels on a one-hour window,
which is what the vocabulary already calls a channel. The journal's
channel table shows the shape: the four kinds a word declares
(`data.channel.gt`, `derived.channel.gt`, `gw.state.machine`,
`gw.weather.channel.gt`) have an Id and a lineage; the three the
journalkeeper declares from hand-coded lists (HeatCallSynth,
WeatherForecast, FloParams) have none, and one of those lists is wrong
today (every FloParams row's display name is "$/MWh x1000", the kWh one
included). `gw.weather.channel.gt` is the precedent: a channel a cloud
GNode declares by a sent record, with Name, Quantity, Unit,
EmitPeriodS, a Start that scopes eras and an Id that changes on a
structural change. `derived.channel.gt` stays the scada's: its
CreatedByNodeName is a layout node and its kind is defined as
layout-declared.

   Why these names. DeclaringServiceAlias follows the registry's

   an instance is about some other kind of node. Start takes ISO

## Open

- The lineage reader takes a second's value from its latest reading
  where the per-series reader averaged the readings in that second;
  where two readings land in one second (five of beech's `dist-swt`
  seconds on 2026-01-09 01:00) an hour's `dist_kwh` moves by about 2%.
  The latest reading is the lineage rule; left as is unless a reader
  needs the within-second average.
- On each roll day two rows of one channel report at once for minutes
  (beech 2026-10-28 23:28 to the old row's last reading; the two rows
  disagree, 58.7 against 36.8 °C on `dist-swt`). The lineage reader
  takes whichever reported last each second, so those hours are not
  comparable with either row alone. Whether these windows are glitch
  rows or the layout's own account of a roll is a question for the
  scada's layout word. The old row also reports once more a day
  after the roll (beech 10-30 14Z, elm 10-28 17Z), one hour where the
  two rows disagree again; and the journalkeeper reactivates a retired
  row when its (name, unit, unit type) returns, so oak, maple and
  spruce's 10-23 blip rows are their roll rows.
- Whether the sema vocabulary gains a per-unit record stating base
  unit and scale, retiring the generator's name convention and map. A
  sema question with the spec first; raised separately, not in this
  design.
- A $/MWh price is not an amount of money, yet the two price channels
  (`lmp-usd-per-mwh`, `total-usd-per-mwh`) record in `DollarsX1000`,
  whose quantity is Currency, so `unit_scale` gives them base unit USD
  and the per-MWh lives in the channel name. `gw1.unit` has no price
  unit; `market.quantity.unit` is the right neighbourhood. A sema
  question, raised separately.
- A time-format convention to add to `conventions/sema-CLAUDE.md`
  "Registry rules" at distill, beside the sender's-clock bullet:
  telemetry timestamps take integer seconds or milliseconds
  (`utc.seconds`, `utc.milliseconds`); a declared era boundary (a
  channel record's Start, a station registration) takes
  `utc.iso8601.seconds`, because a person writes it once and reads it
  by eye. The weather words follow this; `data.channel.gt`'s `StartS`
  converges at its next version. Not a sema spec matter.
- Beech and oak reported their tank and buffer depths as Fahrenheit
  × 100 under the `WaterTempCTimes1000` channel for about a day before
  the 2026-01-09 rename; the join reads those hours by their label.
  Accepted as a data glitch and recorded in the executor's
  `data-glitches.md`; the journal is not rewritten, since the
  eventstore is the record.
