# Knifes Edge — decision brief

**For:** Todd Griset · Matt Polstein
**From:** GridWorks (Jessica Millar)
**Date:** 2026-05-29

## Project

Matt Polstein's Knifes Edge development in Millinocket, Maine —
phase 1 of 14 homes, eventually ~100. **Versant Bangor Hydro
District** (Penobscot County). Phase-1 capital decision is now;
phase-2-through-100 economics differ because of likely AWHP price
decline over the build-out window.

## What we're choosing between

Four design axes — full content in [`design-options.md`](design-options.md):

- **Heat source** — resistive ± minisplit / AWHP for AC, or AWHP
  sized for heating. No propane.
- **Storage** — none / 119-gal tank / store-under-floor A (50 kWh) /
  B (155 kWh).
- **Envelope** — Nolan-house high-R reference + 2–3 graded R-value
  reductions.
- **Electricity product** — Versant standard residential /
  **HOA-as-single-customer + heat-as-service** (recommended) /
  §3351 microgrid fallback. Coop / muni paths ruled out.

## Recommended path (Todd's read, 2026-05-28 meeting)

**HOA-as-single-customer + heat-as-service** under
**35-A MRSA §102(20-B)** (landlord-tenant / abutting-parcel exemption
from "transmission and distribution utility"). HOA buys bundled
retail electricity from Versant as a single commercial customer;
owns the heating equipment + the internal "second conduit"
distribution; sells heat-as-a-service to homeowners at flat annual
or HDD-indexed rates. The §3351 minimal-microgrid is contingency
fallback if §102 fails.

## Decision for Matt now — lay the second conduit

**Recommend yes.** Matt-estimated **~$6k for ~3,000 ft** in the
already-planned trench.

- The trench is being dug. **Marginal cost** of a second conduit is
  conduit + pulling labor only.
- **Without it:** any future second-distribution scheme requires
  re-trenching at multiples of the original cost.
- **With it:** HOA-as-single-customer (option 2) and §3351 microgrid
  (option 3) both become physically possible without re-trenching.
  Optionality is preserved without committing to either path.
- Todd took this seriously at the 2026-05-28 meeting; it is a small
  near-term cost that buys a large legal/structural option.

## J — The §102(20-B) hook, extended

Statute: **35-A MRSA §102(20-B)** excludes from "T&D utility" an
entity distributing electricity through privately-owned property
solely for itself, its tenants, or commercial / industrial
consumers on the **same or abutting** parcel. Customer-protection
implementation: MPUC Chapter 320.

Two questions to nail down:

- **"Tenants" — does the HOA-as-landlord theory survive?** Fee-simple
  homeowners aren't tenants. The cleanest read is an HOA-as-landlord
  master-lease structure with recorded electricity-distribution
  easements over each lot. Whether MPUC has tested an
  HOA-as-landlord-analog claim is open — no public record found via
  open-web search; would need a direct MPUC CMS query.
- **"Abutting" — strict construction.** Internal *public* roads
  break the exemption parcel-by-parcel. If the development's
  internal roads are private (developer → HOA), preserved. **This
  is a regulatory decision hidden in the site-plan submittal and is
  load-bearing for the legal viability.**

Fallback if §102 fails: §3351 minimal-microgrid petition. Statute
sets no minimum island-mode capability; MPUC has not adopted
implementing rules; the practical move is a petition with a
stipulated minimal-island definition that the petitioner proposes
and the PUC adopts in the order.

Coop/muni formation under Title 35-A Ch. 35 (MRRA / Brunswick
Landing model) is technically available as a deeper fallback but
**ruled out** by Todd and Matt at the 2026-05-28 meeting on
regulatory-burden and timeline grounds.

Cross-references: [`regulatory-precedent.md`](regulatory-precedent.md),
[`todd-griset.md`](todd-griset.md).
