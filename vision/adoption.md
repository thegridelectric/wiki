# How people join — field of dreams, not bilateral deals

Status: Draft · Pass 0 · Updated 2026-06-09

> What this is: the GridWorks stance on *how* participants join — by adopting
> open tools and published rules, not by negotiating leverage into bilateral
> deals. A sibling strand to [`ecosystem.md`](ecosystem.md) (why we want an
> ecosystem and what kind of businesses): this one is about the *mechanism* of
> joining. The vision is Jessica's; the framing here is distilled (2026-06-09).

## North star, held lightly

These are orientation, not promises — and deliberately **not** grandiose. Use
them to choose between designs; don't inflate them into a manifesto. The test is
always concrete and small: *does this make it easier for an independent party to
join without asking anyone's permission or out-negotiating anyone?* If a design
passes that test it serves the vision; the soaring language is unnecessary.

## Field of dreams — publish the market and its rules

"If you build it, they will come." A MarketMaker advertises plainly: **here is
the market, here are its rules — go.** Joining means joining a published,
rule-bound market, not striking a private agreement. The rules are visible,
fixed, and the same for everyone who shows up.

Most bilateral energy agreements spend their energy on *who holds leverage*. A
published market with fixed, public rules removes that negotiation — there is no
table to be the stronger party at. This is the same anti-extraction move as
[`ecosystem.md`](ecosystem.md)'s "no huge upside," seen from the entry side:
greed and competition mostly enter through the bilateral deal, so we remove the
deal.

## Agreement embedded in the tools

The open-source tools are built so that **using them is the act of agreeing** to
the shared principles. Building out your slice of the shared **GNode Tree** is,
concretely, agreeing to the shared GNodeAlias map and the market rules attached
to it — there is no separate contract to sign, because the cooperation lives in
the shape of the tool. A map one company keeps is a database; a map many build
together with the same tool is infrastructure — because most utilities cannot
see their own low-voltage network, and even when they can, it is hard to share
(see [`transactive-grid.md`](transactive-grid.md)).

This is the mycelial topology made operational: no center that must grant
access, each node contributing the slice it can see, coordination through shared
protocol rather than through a gatekeeper.

## Open

- How are published rules **fixed and versioned** so "the rules" are trustworthy
  without a central enforcer? (Ties to the MarketMaker as authoritative
  advertiser and to the `market.product` Sema type.)
- Where is the honest line between **embedded agreement and enforcement** — what
  stops a participant from adopting the tools but defecting on the principles,
  and how much of that do we *want* to prevent vs. leave to the SLA / clawback?
- Which existing writing best states this for partners we bring in? Candidates:
  *Redefining Demand Response*, and the field-of-dreams framing above.
