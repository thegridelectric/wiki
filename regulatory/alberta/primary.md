## Settlement framework

Alberta has an hourly real-time pool price (AESO) that a retailer
may pass through to a customer. Load settlement is governed by AUC
Rule 021. Two clauses matter for a site below the profiling cap:

- §3.3(2): the Wire Service Provider (WSP) provides an interval
  meter on request. Quote not captured; read before citing.
- §3.4(2): a site with an interval meter that is not in the class
  load-research sample is settled on its own interval data. Quote
  not captured; read before citing.

## FortisAlberta

**Option I: Interval Metering Option** — rate schedule
"Rates, Options and Riders Schedules", effective July 1, 2026,
page 32 of the PDF (page 29 by the schedule's own numbering).

> "Approved in AUC Decision: 30274-D01-2025 … Effective Date:
> January 1, 2026 … available for Points of Service with a Contract
> Minimum Demand of less than 333 kW (which coincides with an
> Expected Peak Capacity of less than 500 kW), with interval meters,
> to enable 15-minute interval data"

> "Service Charge · Daily · $1.158823 /day"

FortisAlberta's Stakeholder Relations Manager confirmed by email on
2026-09-09:

- A site on Option I is settled on its own hourly interval data in
  initial, interim and final settlement runs, and the retailer of
  record is charged pool price against the site's actual hourly
  volumes. Quote: "Yes."
- No residential-specific limit beyond Rule 021. Quote: "We will
  follow the terms outlined in Rule 021 concerning interval
  profiling."
- The AMI 2.0 meter exchange (E660 meters) can pull interval data,
  but "at this phase in the meter exchanges however we aren't using
  interval data for residential billing purposes", and "at this time
  there would still be that Option I cost".

## Wire Service Providers (delivery side)

Residential delivery is a flat per-kWh charge plus a fixed daily
charge at every WSP. ATCO runs a time-of-use pilot for customers
with large controllable loads. ENMAX (Calgary) and EPCOR (Edmonton)
are city-owned, cover over half of provincial customers, have
interval meters on every home, and each owns a retailer. Municipal
utilities (Red Deer, Lethbridge, Ponoka, Fort Macleod and others)
and the rural electrification associations (EQUS the largest) set
their own delivery rates and together serve about 8% of customers.
Customer shares are from research notes; quote not captured, verify
before citing.

## Other provinces, briefly

- **Ontario:** residential load settles on a single zonal price
  averaged across non-dispatchable load, with the Global Adjustment
  applied flat on top.
- **Nova Scotia:** vertically integrated, no wholesale energy market;
  a storage-specific time-of-day tariff with roughly eight night
  hours plus weekends as the low-cost window.
