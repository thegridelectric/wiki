# Heat Geek (UK): corporate, funding, business model and content research

What this is: desk research on Heat Geek (heatgeek.com) as a model for a US open educational/reference presence for GridWorks. Prices, performance and the FY2025 accounts are in [`price-and-accounts.md`](price-and-accounts.md).

Status: Draft · Pass 0 · Updated 2026-09-23

Method and provenance. heatgeek.com returns HTTP 429 to scripted fetches, so its own pages were read from Wayback Machine snapshots (June 2026) with curl. Companies House and YouTube were read directly. Press pages were read through a fetch tool whose extraction model returns quotes; those are marked "(via extractor)" in Sources and should be spot-checked before public citation. Anything not confirmed by a read source is marked **unverified**.

## 1. Corporate history

- **Origin.** A blog, then a YouTube channel. The channel's own page says "Joined Dec 21, 2016". Heat Geek's site: "In December 2016 he launched the Heat Geek YouTube channel". The Next Web says Adam Chapman "launched Heat Geek as a side project back in 2016". A 2024 trade interview has Chapman dating the blog earlier ("15 years since the official launch of the Heat Geek blog"), so the blog predates the channel; exact blog year **unverified**.
- **Founder story.** A working heating engineer: "About ten years ago, Adam, our co-founder and Chief Geek, was installing gas boilers and watching the people around him cut corners."
- **Co-founders of the scaled company.** Press for the 2024 raise names Adam Chapman, Aadil Qureshi (ex Apple and IBM) and Matthew Gunn (ex JP Morgan). Qureshi is now CEO ("Aadil Qureshi, Co-Founder and CEO, Heat Geek", Carrier release, March 2026). Qureshi joined after trying to buy a heat pump himself and meeting Chapman at a trade show (2022).
- **Company structure (Companies House).**
  - HEAT GEEK LTD, no. 11887015, incorporated 18 March 2019, SIC 63990 "Other information service activities". Original directors Adam Chapman and Patrick Wheeler (Wheeler resigned 17 Jan 2023). Chapman held 50–75% until 24 Jan 2023.
  - HEAT GEEK GROUP LTD, no. 14316058, incorporated 24 Aug 2022 as **SKOON ENERGY GROUP LTD**, renamed 28 Oct 2024. Since 24 Jan 2023 it owns 75%+ of Heat Geek Ltd. Directors: Qureshi (from Aug 2022), Chapman (Jan 2023), Kristian Branaes and Ed Broussard (Oct 2023). Matthew Gunn resigned Oct 2023.
  - HEAT GEEK TECHNOLOGIES LTD, no. 14317488, incorporated 25 Aug 2022 as SKOON ENERGY LTD, SIC 62012 software.
  - HEAT GEEK INSTALLATIONS LTD, no. 14797942, the MCS-certified contracting entity ("MCS: IAA10057", site footer).
  - Reading: Qureshi's heat pump startup Skoon acquired control of Chapman's Heat Geek in January 2023 and rebranded under the Heat Geek name in October 2024. The acquisition reading is inferred from the filings, not from a press statement.
- **Headcount.** Tracxn: "32 employees (as of August 31, 2026)". **Unverified** (aggregator estimate). The careers page describes "a team of heating engineers, educators, developers and a dedicated operations team".

## 3. Business model

Heat Geek describes itself as "a platform built by engineers, for engineers". It also says "We are not a franchise and we are not an employer." Revenue lines, as of the June 2026 site:

1. **Training subscription ("Heat Geek Learn").** "Learn £250/mo Excl. VAT", covering "Advanced heating design courses", "MCS Accredited", "Ongoing support and manufacturer training". Earlier price: "£150 + VAT per month" (Which? Trusted Traders offer page). Courses: Enlightenment, Awakening, Mastery, Optimising. Accreditation: "accredited by NOCN, giving you a nationally recognised Level 3 heat pump installer qualification regulated by Ofqual and accepted for MCS certification."
2. **Installer platform ("Upgrades").** "Upgrades £350/mo Excl. VAT" includes "iPad Pro + Custom Heat Geek Apps", "Leads and tools to sell yourself", "Technical and Admin support". Heat Geek takes the admin ("Customers buy and we take care of all back-office work like MCS, DNO and BUS applications."), ships parts ("We ship all the parts to site on the first day of install"), and pays the installer ("Get payment for your work within 10 days"). The installer sets labour rates and "own[s] the customer relationship". So Heat Geek Installations Ltd is the MCS contractor of record and the local installer works as a subcontractor. Heat Geek's margin on the install sale is **unverified**.
3. **Homeowner sales.** A "£250 Design Consultation Fee (inc. VAT)" with "AI-system design" and an "Instant customisable proposal", plus a £500 renovation design package. The claim is "£3,000 average price" after the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant ("up to £9,000 Grant … do all the paperwork and apply it to your bill"). A "Heat Geek Guarantee" of performance runs "for 5 years after your system is commissioned". "Heat Geek Black Label" (20% off for renovations) and "ZeroDisrupt" (low-disruption install process) are productised offers.
4. **Lead referral from utilities.** Octopus (June 2024) gives Heat Geek installers "the ability to receive leads from Octopus". OVO ran an earlier partnership (launched Sept 2023, per 2024 funding press); the site's own article says it "may have ended" (via search snippet, **unverified**).
5. **Manufacturer relationships.** "Ongoing support and manufacturer training" is in the Learn tier. Carrier is now an investor. Paid sponsorship deals are **unverified**.

**How free content feeds paid lines.** Heat Geek tells the funnel itself: "The channel became the training courses. The courses became a badge of honour … The training became a community … a network". Free YouTube and articles build trust with engineers, who pay for Learn. Learn graduates who pass the competency check get the Upgrades platform. Homeowners arrive through the same content ("You've done the research, now get your instant quote" sits inside the Knowledge Hub list) and become leads for the network. The badge ("Heat Geek Elite", "Mastery") is shown on installers' own sites, so the brand also spreads through installer marketing.

## 4. Scale

- **YouTube** (read 2026-09-23): "78.1K subscribers", "8,323,511 views", "280 videos". Heat Geek's about page (June 2026): "75,000 YouTube Subscribers And over 4 million views". A third-party blog claims "around 200k+ subscribers". That is contradicted by the channel itself and is likely wrong.
- **Engineers trained.** The figure has risen over time: "1,000 trained installers" (funding press, early 2024), "Over 2,000 heating engineers" (Octopus, June 2024), "Over 3,000 engineers have now passed them" (become-a-Heat-Geek page, 2026), and "4,000 Installers Have taken the Heat Geek training course" (about page, June 2026). These are take/pass counts, not network members. The size of the verified network (Upgrades members) is **unverified**.
- **Homes.** "130,000+ Homes assessed" (about page). That counts assessments, not installs. Install count is **unverified**.
- **Monitoring.** In May 2024 the company had "access to live data from 41 heat pumps and are planning to add another 76". The 2026 site says "We are connected to every heat pump we install".
- **Targets.** "bringing on board as many as 10,000 more installers in the next few years" (The Next Web, May 2024).

## 5. Content strategy

- **Voice and authorship.** Articles are practitioner-written. The Wayback author index shows named engineer-authors (adamheatgeek, patrickheatgeek, andrewmoffat, andyplant, john_cassells, and others). The Knowledge Hub says it is "written by the people who know them best". The tone is plain, opinionated and physics-first ("teaching the physics of moving heat", Fully Charged). Brand values include "We celebrate the rabbit-holing on flow temperatures".
- **Two audiences, one hub.** The Knowledge Hub (18 pages of posts in June 2026) mixes engineer technique ("Balancing", "The Knowledge") with consumer and SEO pieces dated 2026 ("Green mortgages", "Future Homes Standard", "Energy Price Cap", "Landlord Heat Pump Grants"). Maintenance looks like continuous new posts plus year-stamped guides ("Heat Pump Guide 2026"). The older technical corpus stays up (a 2022 post was still featured).
- **Licensing: proprietary.** The website terms say "All intellectual property in the Platform belongs to us or our licensors" and "You may not modify or remove copyright or legal notices". No Creative Commons licence was found. Courses are paid and gated.
- **Open data.** No evidence that Heat Geek publishes its fleet data openly. The UK open-data role is played by OpenEnergyMonitor's HeatpumpMonitor.org: "an OpenEnergyMonitor open source community initiative to share and compare heat pump performance data", with over 600 systems. Individual Heat Geek installers may list systems there (**unverified**). Heat Geek's own "AI" design tool (ZeroDisrupt) is described as using real-world data, but it is closed.

## 6. UK policy and industry relationships

- **Boiler Upgrade Scheme.** Central to the homeowner offer: Heat Geek applies the grant to the bill and handles "MCS, DNO and BUS applications" for installers.
- **MCS.** Heat Geek Installations Ltd is itself MCS certified (IAA10057) and acts as an umbrella for its installers. Courses are "accepted for MCS certification".
- **Ofqual/NOCN.** Its Level 3 qualification makes the training fit the formal skills route. Whether Heat Geek courses are eligible for DESNZ's £500 Heat Training Grant is **unverified**.
- **Octopus.** A trusted partner in Octopus's installer programme since 27 June 2024.
- **OVO.** Earlier installation partnership (see section 3).
- **Nesta.** Soft ties only. Heat Geek promoted a Nesta installer survey (LinkedIn), and Nesta's June 2026 blog cites "Heat Geek's Zero Disrupt" as an example of lower-disruption installs. No Nesta funding was found.
- **Government funding.** "£600,000 in the form of grants" to May 2024, funder **unverified**.

## 7. US expansion and US analogues (brief)

- **Heat Geek in the US.** Nothing found. The Carrier release frames the investment as "helping accelerate residential heat pump adoption across Europe", and the "US Mention" check came back "None". The site says "Heat Geek trained engineers are now working across the world", which suggests reach through online courses, not a US operation.
- **US analogues.** None combines all three pieces (content, accredited training, installer network with leads and admin):
  - **HeatingHelp.com** (Dan Holohan lineage; Holohan not named on the page read): hydronics forum "The Wall", "Find a Contractor" listings, books. Closest in audience (hydronic/steam, Northeast). Content and directory, not a platform.
  - **HVAC School** (Bryan Orr): "free training HVAC/R … For Techs, By Techs", plus a podcast and paid courses. Air-side, tech-focused, no homeowner funnel.
  - **Energy Vanguard** (Allison Bailes): building-science blog, HVAC design and load-calc services. Closest in tone to Heat Geek's articles.
  - **HeatSpring**: paid online courses including air-to-water heat pumps with hydronic distribution.
- No US player combines a hydronic air-to-water focus, a performance guarantee, monitoring and a leads platform. That matches GridWorks's niche in New England.

## What this means for GridWorks

- Heat Geek grew from one engineer's credible, physics-first free content, built over about seven years before any institutional money. The community and trust existed before the platform. A US version needs a real practitioner voice and a long time horizon. A marketing channel will not do the same job.
- Its money comes from installers (a training subscription, then a platform subscription) and from owning the install sale as the MCS contractor of record. Content is proprietary and serves as the top of that funnel. If GridWorks publishes openly (CC-licensed, open data), it gives up that funnel and must find the value somewhere else, such as dispatch revenue, the MarketMaker, or partner utilities.
- The open-data layer in the UK is a separate community project (HeatpumpMonitor.org), not Heat Geek. GridWorks's measured fleet data and transactive dispatch results would fill a gap that no one fills in the US, and that neither UK player covers for thermal storage or grid value.
- The US gap is hydronic air-to-water design knowledge for cold climates. HeatingHelp has the audience and Energy Vanguard has the tone, but neither teaches air-to-water with storage and controls. GridWorks's existing "Electrifying Hydronic Heating in New England" series is a natural seed.
- Policy leverage mattered: the BUS grant, MCS umbrella status and an Ofqual-regulated qualification made Heat Geek part of the formal system. The US equivalents to look at are Mass Save and Efficiency Maine contractor networks, state workforce-training grants, and IRA-era rebates.
- Utilities send Heat Geek demand (Octopus, OVO). A New England utility or muni partner such as Hingham could play the Octopus role for a GridWorks-trained installer cohort.
